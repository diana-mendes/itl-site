Vision

Tyres are a
material, not
a waste stream

Every end-of-life tyre is a dense package of engineered carbon, polymer and steel. Today most of it is burned or shredded into low-value filler. Recovering it as chemistry is where this ends up — and it is why we are starting where we are.

The problem

1bn+

Tyres reach end of life worldwide each year.

~70%

Are burned for fuel, landfilled, or downcycled into crumb rubber.

Tonnes

Of recoverable carbon black and oil lost in every batch that goes to incineration.

Our approach

Conventional tyre recycling is a blunt instrument. Shredding produces crumb rubber worth a fraction of the material that went in. Pyrolysis recovers carbon and oil, but at high temperature, with variable quality and a significant energy penalty.

We work at the other end of the scale: selective, low-temperature chemistry that separates a tyre into its constituent materials without destroying them. The key is the solvent — and finding the right solvent is a search problem, which is where our models come in.

In practice

Recover

Materials, not fuel

We target recovery of carbon black, polymer fractions and steel in forms clean enough to sell back into manufacturing — not just to burn.

Design

Solvents fit to purpose

Designer solvents are tuned to the bond we want to break. That selectivity is what keeps recovered material at high value.

Scale

Built for industry

Processes designed from the outset around feedstock that is dirty, variable, and arrives by the tonne.

Why it matters

Carbon black is made by burning fossil feedstock, and it is one of the most carbon-intensive commodity materials in the tyre. Recovering it rather than making it again displaces that production directly.

The same applies down the line: every kilogram of polymer and steel returned to industry at usable quality is a kilogram that does not need to be made from virgin material. Circularity here is not a label — it is a materials balance.

End-of-life tyres are the hardest version of this problem and the biggest prize. We are not starting there. We are starting with clean, single-source production scrap, where the chemistry is tractable today and the commercial case for manufacturers is immediate — and building toward the tyre.
