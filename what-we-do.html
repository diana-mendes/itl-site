Why scrap, and why now

Production scrap is the part of the rubber waste problem that is already solved on paper and unsolved in practice. You know exactly what is in it, because you made it. It has not been contaminated, weathered, or mixed with four other manufacturers' compounds. It is sitting on one site, in known volumes, on a predictable schedule.

That is precisely what makes it tractable. Recovery chemistry that would take years to qualify against mixed post-consumer feedstock can be validated far faster against a stream whose composition is documented from the start — and certification time is usually the binding constraint, not the chemistry.

Meanwhile the same material is costing you money to remove. Disposal is a line item; recovered feedstock is an input you would otherwise buy at market price.

On the regulatory side

EUDR brings natural rubber into the EU Deforestation Regulation's scope, with due-diligence and traceability obligations on the material entering your supply chain. Recovered content from your own production scrap carries an origin you can already evidence.

Recycled content targets and virgin material reduction are moving the same direction. Material you recover on your own site is the cleanest version of that story to tell — and to audit.
